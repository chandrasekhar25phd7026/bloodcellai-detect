What is this blood-cell dataset, really?

Point it at a folder of microscope images and label files. It works out the annotation format and what the dataset is for, and — for the formats it actually knows how to convert — can harmonize it into a ready-to-train YOLO export. Entirely in your browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere. When it can't tell, or can't harmonize, it says so plainly instead of guessing.

About this tool, and what it doesn't do
  • Two detectors run on every dataset. The frozen detector (v1.4) is the exact rule set behind the published BloodCellAI results — it is not edited here. The experimental check (v1.5) adds folder-name and image-pairing evidence for segmentation datasets that v1.4 can only see by file name; it was tuned on two real datasets (Raabin-WBC and a Zheng et al. white-blood-cell set), so a result on those two is not a fair, held-out test of it.
  • Harmonization covers five formats. For VOC XML, COCO JSON, YOLO text, point labels, and classification folders, it can build a harmonized YOLO export (class names discovered from your data, remapped to contiguous ids). There is no harmonizer yet for segmentation masks or custom polygon/bounding-box JSON, and it says so rather than guessing. Everything is exported under a single "train" split; this tool does not assign train/val/test splits.
  • Point-label boxes are a disclosed heuristic. A labeled point has no natural box size, so one is drawn as a fixed fraction of each image's own real width, not a hard-coded pixel size — still a convention, not a standard, and worth checking against your actual cell sizes.
  • Nothing leaves your browser. Files are read locally to list names and, for a small sample of label files and images, their contents; none of it is sent anywhere. This matters if your dataset has a restrictive licence.
  • It can abstain, deliberately. When no rule finds a confident pattern, it reports "not detected" rather than guessing. That is a correct, informative outcome — not a bug.
  • Some image formats can't be decoded in a browser. TIFF masks, for instance, can't be opened by most browsers' image decoders, so the experimental check falls back to folder-name and pairing evidence alone for those, and says so.
  • Very large selections: file names are scanned in full (up to 20,000 files); only a small sample of label files and images is actually read.
Runs entirely client-side. Source rules ported from the BloodCellAI project's frozen v1.4 detector and its experimental v1.5 extension.
